### 3.15 The context budget — the documents as a working surface

**Not a build-order step.** §5's table is what gets built; this is about what it costs to consult
the eleven documents while building it, and it runs alongside rather than between the steps.

**Measured first, across 23 session transcripts, 2026-08-08 → 08-09.** Governance documents are
**77% of all bytes read** (3,035 KB of 3,939 KB). Median read before the first edit: **137 KB
≈ 34k tokens**; worst session 383 KB. Three premises did not survive the measurement — the figure
was believed to be ~150k (it is ~4× smaller), sessions were believed to compact early (**no
transcript carries a compaction marker**, including one of 1,128 messages), and documents were
believed to be read whole (**95% of reads already use `offset`/`limit`**).

**What the measurement actually found.** The distribution is bimodal: reads over 15 KB are **19% of
reads but 55% of the bytes**. Section sizes are p50 4.6 KB and p90 11 KB — already consultable —
with **one outlier, `decisions-ledger.md` §3 at 183.5 KB**, 79% of this file. Cost per citation puts the
same finding the other way: `plan.md` is 0.18 KB per citation, this document is **7.64** — largest,
least cited, 42× the cost. The corpus is consulted well; one section is not consultable at all.

**And it grows.** `__internal__` went 790 KB → 969 KB across the five S3/S3b commits in a single
day. Between the measurement above and the first commit against it, two more commits added **17 KB,
97% of it into §3** — and one of the two was a pure rename with no new behaviour (**D-161**).

**D-162 · The eleven documents get a generated anchor index, and a stale one fails the build**
— **Decision.** `__internal__/INDEX.md` carries every `##`, `###` and `####` in the corpus — 489
sections — with its anchor as a citation writes it, its line range and its size. Grouped by
document, with a summary table giving each block's start line, so resolving a citation costs the
~1.5 KB summary plus one ~3 KB block rather than the file's 34.7 KB. Generated by `pnpm
docs:index`, compared byte-for-byte by `check:doc-index` in the `verify` job, and regenerated in
the same commit as any document change (`definition-of-done.md` rule 1.11).
— **Rejected.** *Split the ten reasoning documents into §-addressable files* — a `##` is already
4.6 KB at p50 and a targeted read returns 5.6 KB, so it buys ~0 tokens while putting 1,598
§-citations through a rewrite; the granularity is broken in exactly one place, and that is D-163's
subject. *Write a retrieval discipline into `CLAUDE.md` as a rule* — 95% of reads already follow
it, so the rule would add text to the one file loaded unconditionally and change nothing; making
each section's size visible does the same work with no rule at all. *Directory-scoped `CLAUDE.md`
files* — per-package copies must restate §0 and the port rule in each, for a ≤4k token ceiling.
*A generated digest of the rules* — a second statement of every rule by construction, and a digest
of an argument is the skim `CLAUDE.md`'s three-surface split already rejects.
— **Effect.** A citation resolves to an exact range, so an oversized read has no excuse and
`§3`'s 183.5 KB is legible as the outlier it is. Costs one generated file, one check, and rule
1.11's obligation on every document change. It carries **no rule text** — only heading titles — so
the three-surface split holds by construction rather than by review. It is also the precondition
for the two steps after it, both of which cite anchors.
— **Settled.** 2026-08-09, by `check:doc-index` passing in both directions — proved to fail on an
inserted heading and on this session's own edits to `testing.md` and `definition-of-done.md` — plus
17 unit tests, lint, and the 236-test unit project.
— **Reasoning.** `testing.md` §8.1 owns the check — input, algorithm, failure output and its three
blind spots. This entry owns only that the index exists and why the four alternatives lost.

**One figure the shard was planned against was wrong, and it is recorded rather than quietly
fixed.** The step was sized at *"81 `§3.N` references inside `decisions-ledger.md`"* to re-point. Counted
at this gate: **110 occurrences, and 105 of them cite another document** — `legal.md` §3.3,
`definition-of-done.md` §3.1, `plan.md` §3.4 — leaving **five** that point at a sibling ledger
entry. Re-pointing is five links, not eighty-one, and rewriting the other 105 would have broken
every one of them.

**D-163 · Each `§3.N` entry becomes one file under `__internal__/decisions/`, and the index
recurses to find them**
— **Decision.** The fifteen entries move to `decisions/3.NN-<slug>.md`, **verbatim** — 199
paragraphs carried, of which three gained a markdown link around a sibling anchor and changed in
no other way. Zero-padded, so the directory sorts in ledger order and **the file name is the
anchor**: `§3.13` is `decisions/3.13-…`. Each keeps its `### 3.N` heading, so what the corpus cites
is still a real heading in a real file. §3 becomes a fifteen-row table — anchor, file, D-range,
verdict — and §0–§2 and §4–§7 stay inline at 0.7–10.5 KB each.
`readIndexableDocuments` now walks `__internal__` recursively, and `renderIndex`'s existing *no
numbered heading, no block* rule decides what is indexed.
— **Rejected.** *Leave the index reading top-level `.md` only* — the fifteen entries drop out of
`INDEX.md` silently, `§3.13` resolves to nothing, and the index claims a corpus it does not cover;
that is the failure D-162 built it to prevent. *Name `decisions/` in the script instead of
recursing* — a check carrying its own copy of the file list keeps passing after a sixteenth entry,
which is the exact defect `readIndexableDocuments` exists to avoid, one level down. *Skip the
subdirectories by name so the ten design notes stay out* — the same list-in-the-script defect;
they stay out because they carry no numbered heading, which is also why nothing cites them.
*Promote each entry to `##`* — the anchor stops being a `###`, and every entry then needs an
exemption from a ceiling written against `##` sections. *Summarise the entries into the table* —
a ledger that condenses is the digest D-162 already rejected. The wider alternatives — sharding
the other ten documents, a retrieval rule, a rules digest — are **D-162's Rejected line**, and are
not re-argued here.
— **Effect.** The largest `##` section in the corpus goes **187.4 KB → 21.9 KB** (`legal.md` §3),
and `decisions-ledger.md` **226.7 KB → 40.1 KB**. The largest ledger unit reachable is §3.14 at 59.7 KB
and it is now opt-in rather than the cost of opening the file. **The growth vector is what this
buys:** the next step appends a file instead of 20–60 KB into the middle of the corpus's largest
section, and `decisions-ledger.md` stops growing with the build. Costs fifteen files, fifteen rows in
`INDEX.md`'s summary, and one more hop to reach an entry.
— **Settled.** 2026-08-09, at this gate. Verified against the pre-shard file paragraph by
paragraph — 199 carried, 3 differing and all three only by an added link; **no `§` citation lost**
anywhere in the corpus, counted as a multiset before and after; the one external `§3.N` citation
re-pointed (`docs-site.md` §2.1); `check:doc-index` green over 36 files read and 26 indexed, and
proved to fail on an edit to a sharded entry.
— **Reasoning.** `testing.md` §8.1 owns what the index reads and why its selection rule is a
heading count rather than a path list. This entry owns only that the entries are files.

**What the first two steps did not touch.** Both cut the cost of *one* lookup. Neither touches the
**routing** cost: a task needing four documents still pays four grep-then-read cycles to find out
which four. That saving is an **estimate** — −30 to −55 KB on a task that fits a bundle, ~0 on one
that does not — and it is labelled as such against the two measured figures above.

**D-164 · Five task-shaped skills carry anchors and never line ranges, and a check resolves every
pointer**
— **Decision.** `.agents/skills/` gains `port-a-component`, `attribution`, `add-a-check`,
`docs-page` and `close-a-step`: 29–32 lines each, every line naming a section to read rather than
what it says. Each is checked by `check:skill-pointers` in the `verify` job, which resolves every
`§`, every backticked path and every script name, and enforces the shape — a citation on every
body line, ≤14 words beside it, ≤40 lines. `skills-lock.json` is untouched at three entries; it is
the list of *vendored* skills, and it is therefore also how the check knows which directories are
ours.
— **Rejected.** *Line ranges written into the skill*, as the step was originally drafted — a range
in a hand-written file is invalidated by any edit above it in the document it points at, which is
the whole reason `INDEX.md` is generated and rule 1.11 exists; five files carrying ~40 ranges each
have no generator, so every document commit would stale them silently and the repair would be by
hand. *Generating the skills too* — the reading **order** is a judgement about a task, not a
property of the corpus, and a generator has no source for it. *Leaving the pointer-versus-summary
boundary to review alone* — it is the one failure of this step that is green, useful-looking and
wrong, so it gets the structural half of a proxy and §7.8 gets the rest. *Folding the check into
`check:doc-index`* — same corpus, different subject: one says *regenerate the index*, the other
*re-read this pointer*, and `pnpm docs:index` repairs nothing in a skill. The wider alternatives —
sharding the ten documents, a retrieval rule in `CLAUDE.md`, directory-scoped `CLAUDE.md` files, a
rules digest — are **D-162's Rejected line** and are not re-argued here.
— **Effect.** A task opens with one file of ~1 KB instead of four grep-then-read cycles, and the
anchors it names resolve through `INDEX.md` at read time. Costs five files, one check, 24 unit
tests, and rule 1.12's obligation on any commit that renumbers a section or moves a script. It
carries **no rule text**, so the three-surface split holds; the part of that which is not
mechanical is `definition-of-done.md` §7.8, written the way rule 7.7 was. `CLAUDE.md` gains one
pointer and goes 15.4 → 15.6 KB, inside the 16 KB ceiling the next step sets.
— **Settled.** 2026-08-09, at this gate. `check:skill-pointers` proved in both directions — green
over 5 skills and 72 pointer lines, and shown to fail on a broken anchor, an absent file, an
absent script, a line citing nothing, and a paragraph beside a citation. The 14-word ceiling is
measured, not chosen: the widest genuine line across the five carries 11. Census re-taken
mechanically, 45/20/25/0 → **46/21/25/0**.
— **Reasoning.** `testing.md` §8.2 owns the check — input, the citation grammar, algorithm, failure
output and its three blind spots. This entry owns only that the skills exist, and that they carry
anchors rather than ranges.


**Two things D-162 left behind were found here, and are recorded rather than quietly fixed.**
`CLAUDE.md`'s census row still read **44/19/25/0**, the pre-index count — §7b was re-taken and the
number was not carried to the second place it is written. And `testing.md` §12's `verify` row never
gained `check:doc-index`, so the CI job map has been describing a job with one fewer check in it
than the workflow runs. Both are now correct, and both are the same shape as §7b's own complaint
one level up: a figure or a list presented as current that nothing re-derives. Neither has a check,
and naming one would add a twenty-sixth unwritten script for something two greps disprove.


**The three steps above are guarded by nothing, and two of the three savings are estimates.** What
closes this entry is the check that measures the corpus instead of trusting it — and, in the same
script, the one that re-measures the bill instead of believing the figure the plan opened with.

**D-165 · A 25 KB ceiling on every citable unit, a 16 KB one on `CLAUDE.md`, and an allow-list row
that fails when it stops being needed**
— **Decision.** `check:context-budget`, in the `verify` job. Three units, because a citation costs
whatever the unit it names costs: a `##` section, a **sharded file** (whose heading is a `###`, so
the section rule never sees it), and `CLAUDE.md`. Exceptions are rows in `context-budget.config.ts`
— file, **the size the row was granted at**, reason, and what deletes it — and three separate
faults come out of it: over the ceiling with no row, grown past the granted size, and a row that is
no longer needed. `--sessions` is the same script's transcript half, a local diagnostic registered
as `definition-of-done.md` §7.9. Sizes are **UTF-8 bytes** throughout, measured by the function
`INDEX.md` is generated with.
— **Rejected.** *Raise the ceiling to fit the three tight cases* — `legal.md` §3 at 22.1 KB,
`decisions/3.13` at 23.3, `CLAUDE.md` at 15.6 — a ceiling fitted to what already exists is a
ceiling that gets fitted again, which is a deleted one; 25 KB is 2.3× the corpus's p90 and it is
the number the measurement produced, so the three pass and are **named on every green run with
their headroom** instead. *Fire on growth past the ceiling in one commit rather than on absolute
size* — a one-shot: six commits of 1 KB cross it and nothing ever fires again, and incremental
growth is the exact failure D-162's measurement found. *A markdown table in `definition-of-done.md`
for the allow-list, like §5 and §6* — those two are prose because their checks do not exist and a
person is their only reader; this one has a machine reader today, and a hand-parsed table puts a
second grammar between the rule and its enforcement whose characteristic failure is a malformed row
skipped rather than reported.
*A second `check:*` for the transcript half* — a name CI can never run is what §7b exists to stop,
so it is a flag and the census does not move for it. *An allow-list row with no size on it* — an
unbounded exception is where growth would go to hide, which is the hole this check exists to close.
— **Effect.** The next 60 KB entry written into a document instead of its own file fails the build,
and so does the next paragraph added to `CLAUDE.md` past 16 KB. Costs one script, two `scripts/lib`
modules, 34 unit tests, one root config file, and rule 1.13's obligation on every commit. **Two
defects were found by writing it, and both were silent.** `parseSections` reported `bytes` that were
JavaScript string length rather than bytes — a 2% under-count on em-dash-heavy text, 550 bytes
against a 25 KB ceiling, and enough for `INDEX.md` and this check to disagree about one section;
D-162's code is fixed and the index's figures moved. And the root `tsconfig.json` sets
`"files": []`, so **no `tsc` pass reads a root config file** — `attribution.config.ts` included — so
the allow-list's shape is validated by the check at runtime rather than trusted to its interface.
— **Settled.** 2026-08-09, at this gate. Proved in both directions — green over 129 sections, 18
shards and one allow-listed file, and shown to fail, against the real corpus, on **six** deliberate
breakages: an oversized `##` section, an unlisted oversized shard, a shard grown past its granted
size, a stale allow-list row, an over-ceiling `CLAUDE.md`, and a row missing a field. Census
re-taken mechanically, 46/21/25/0 → **47/22/25/0**.
— **Reasoning.** `testing.md` §8.3 owns both halves — the three ceilings, where 25 KB comes from,
the allow-list's three faults, and the blind spots. This entry owns only that the ceiling exists
and why the five alternatives lost.

**The headline this entry opens with does not reproduce, and it is recorded rather than quietly
restated.** Run over the transcripts on disk, the transcript half gets **108.6 KB** for the
pre-edit governance median under the definition stated above; widening to every `.md` read gives
114.5 KB and to every read of any file 155.7 KB. **None of the three is 137 KB.** Every *other*
figure re-derives: 76% governance share against 77%, 94% scoped reads against 95%, worst two
387/307 KB against 383/304. So it is the same measurement disagreeing on one number, not a
different one — and the cause is not recoverable, because transcripts are appended to after the
fact and *the 23 files that existed on 08-09* cannot be reconstructed by any ordering available
today. **The consequence is a rule, not a correction:** 137 KB is printed as a quoted figure, the
before/after table is not a subtraction, and the target — **≤ 60 KB** — is what the number is read
against. Today's figure is **86.5 KB over n=25**, and it is not evidence for or against any of
D-162 … D-164: 23 of those 25 sessions predate all three.

**D-166 · `testing.md` §8's check definitions become files under `testing/`, on D-163's mechanism**
— **Decision.** §8.1 and §8.2 move verbatim to `testing/8.01-doc-index.md` and
`testing/8.02-skill-pointers.md`, §8.3 arrives as a third, and §8 keeps its per-script routing table
plus a three-row map. The anchors do not move: `` `testing.md` §8.2 `` still resolves, because
`readIndexableDocuments` recurses and `buildAnchorResolver` folds `<name>/**` into `<name>.md` —
**both written at D-163 as general rules with no path named**, so this shard costs no script change
at all.
— **Rejected.** *Decide it when the check fires* — **it fires on this commit.** §8 was 16.7 KB
carrying two definitions and §8.3 is 10.2 KB, so unsharded the section is ~27.5 KB: over the ceiling
introduced in the same change. `definition-of-done.md` §7b names 25 checks still to come, most
owing a definition of the same size. *An allow-list row for §8* — an
exception granted to the section that grows fastest is the ceiling deleted with extra steps, and
unlike `decisions/3.14` §8 is not a closed record. *Move the definitions to a new document* — a
twelfth reasoning document for material `testing.md` §0 already owns, and the three-surface split
would have to be re-argued. *Shard §1–§7 too* — they are single arguments that do not grow with
the build; §8 is a container of citable units that gains one per check, which is the only property
that made §3 worth sharding.
— **Effect.** §8 goes **16.7 KB → 9.4 KB** and stops tracking the build, so the 25 unwritten checks
land as files rather than into a section. `INDEX.md` goes 26 indexed files → 29 with no code
change, which is the claim D-163 made about its own generality, tested. §7.7's file-naming
convention widens from `decisions/` to any sharded file.
— **Settled.** 2026-08-09, at this gate. `check:doc-index` green over 39 files read and 29 indexed;
`check:skill-pointers` green, so the five skills' `testing.md` anchors still resolve across the
move; no `§` citation in the corpus changed.
— **Reasoning.** `testing.md` §8's own preamble owns why that section is sharded and §1–§7 are not.
This entry owns only that it happened on D-163's mechanism rather than a new one.
