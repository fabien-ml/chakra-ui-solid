### 3.17 S5 — the landing page, and the documents that still described the old one

**Not a build-order step.** The docs home was ported to chakra-ui.com's structure at `362256b` and
the commit was green, because nothing in this repo checks a document against the code it specifies.
Three sections went on saying the opposite of what shipped, and one of them — `docs-site.md` §3.4 —
said the shipped page was not allowed to exist.

**The pattern is D-167's, one phase later.** That entry recorded a sweep that fixed a rule in the two
places it was named and none of the six where it was restated. This one records the same shape from
the other side: a *change* that landed in code and in none of the four places the old rule was
written down. The three-surface split (§0) tells a reader which document owns a claim; it does not
tell the author of a commit which documents just became wrong.

**One of the four was not a stale sentence but a missing check** — D-174. `attribution.config.ts`
could not express what shipped, and the only reason nothing failed is that no check looked at
`apps/`.

**D-176 is the same failure at corpus scale**, found while fixing the first three: `legal.md` was
deleted at `1eb56e8` and 286 citations to it were left pointing at nothing. **A deleted document is
a change to every document that cites it**, and no gate in this repo says so.

**D-177 is the shape from the third side.** Not a change that outran its documents and not a deleted
one, but a claim that was **never measured** — written once in `plan.md` §3.4 at P3 and restated in
five places, all of them green, two of them consumer-facing. It named a function `@pandacss/dev` does
not export, and it warned about the one key of the three that is safe.

**D-173 · Chakra's bolt and favicon are used, attributed as MIT files, and trademark is carried by
four named mitigations instead of a ban** ⟲
— **Decision.** `docs-site.md` §3.4's *no Chakra logo, wordmark, favicon derivative, social card, or
combined mark, anywhere* is retired. Four files come across under the same MIT grant as the rest of
Chakra's source — `BlitzIcon`, `BlitzFillIcon`, the `LogoIcon` glyph, `favicon.ico` — with a registry
entry, an `@license` header and root `NOTICE.md` rows. §3.4 is rewritten as the four mitigations that
actually carry the trademark question: the logotype reads `chakra-ui-solid`, **no wordmark lettering
is reproduced**, no social card or combined mark exists, and the disclaimer is verbatim above the
fold and in every footer.
— **Rejected.** *Keep the ban and redraw the icons* — the ban was written as though the licence
required it, exactly as `legal.md`'s rewrite-every-sentence policy was, and D-148 already measured
that licence: one MIT grant at their repo root, nothing separate under `apps/www`. A redrawn bolt
close enough to read as the same mark would be a **derivative of the mark** and a worse trademark
position than the honest copy with a disclaimer over it. *Ship no mark at all* — considered and it is
a real option; it loses the one thing the glyph does, which is tell a Chakra reader within a second
what this library targets. The disclaimer is what keeps that from becoming a claim. *Keep the ban
and delete the assets* — the same, with the page already built.
— **Effect.** The copyright line moved and the trademark line did not, which is `docs-site.md` §3.4's
own closing point demonstrated rather than asserted: the flag list is a copyright control, §3.4 is
the trademark one, and neither substitutes for the other. **The four mitigations are now written as a
table with a column saying where each one is in the shipped site**, because a mitigation nobody can
locate is a mitigation nobody can check. What would reopen the section is named there too — wordmark
lettering, a combined mark, a social card, or any use that reads as the site's identity.
— **Settled.** S5, 2026-08-10. Reverses **D-90**'s *no Chakra logo, wordmark, or visual identity
anywhere in the chrome*, and the trademark paragraph carried forward unexamined in **D-148**'s
*Effect*.
— **Reasoning.** `docs-site.md` §3.4, §3.2 row 11a; **D-148**.

**D-174 · The attribution registry covers `apps/`, and `package: null` is what an unpublished
derivative owes**
— **Decision.** `AttributionEntry.package` becomes `string | null`. `null` keeps the registry entry,
the `@license` header and the root `NOTICE.md` row; it drops the package `NOTICE.md`, the `files`
array and the `dist/` header check. A second export, **`noticeOnlyPaths`**, declares the six rows
that owe a row and can carry no header — a directory, a binary, four framework logos — each with its
reason. `check:notice-rows`' orphan scan widens from `packages/` to `packages|apps`.
— **Rejected.** *State in `docs-site.md` §3 that the docs app is attributed by NOTICE row alone* —
the honest form of that sentence is *"because the checks were not written for it"*, which is the gap
rather than a reason for it. Two things were deletable with nothing going red: `icons.tsx`'s header
and its row. *Make `package` optional rather than nullable* — a field you can forget is a field that
gets forgotten; `null` has to be typed, so every entry states which kind it is. *Put the logos and
the favicon in the registry too* — the registry's defining assertion is a matching `@license` header,
and there is nowhere in an `.ico` to put one. A list whose members cannot satisfy the check would
have to be special-cased inside it, which is the second list with worse ergonomics.
— **Effect.** Widening the scan **failed on its first run** — `icons.tsx` had no LICENSE pointer and
no *"This file has been modified from the original."* line, both added here. Three break-tests then
confirmed it bites in all three directions: deleting the row fails, an undeclared `apps/` row fails,
and stripping a header line fails. Every row in the root `NOTICE.md` under `packages/` or `apps/` is
now declared in exactly one of the two lists. **The `apps/docs` entry is also the first case of
`CLAUDE.md` obligation 3's *"the only notice a consumer ever sees"* not applying** — the docs app has no
tarball and is published to the web, which is why the row and the header stayed while the three
tarball obligations went.
— **Settled.** S5, 2026-08-10, by running the widened checks against three deliberate breaks.
— **Reasoning.** `docs-site.md` §3.3; `testing.md` §9; `definition-of-done.md` rule 1.7.

**D-175 · The docs home copies chakra-ui.com's structure, minus five sections that are claims about
standing** ⟲
— **Decision.** `docs-plan.md` §3.1's *the one page whose structure is not copied* is retired, and
§3.2's seven-section order is replaced by the five that shipped: hero (with the requirements line and
the disclaimer inside it), design system, parity, frameworks, closing. **Dropped and named:** *used
by*, the stats row, testimonials, sponsors, the Pro tier.
— **Rejected.** *Keep the original frame* — its argument was *a marketing page's structure is its
claims*, and that is false as stated: a hero and a framework grid claim nothing by existing. What is
true of five of their sections is not true of the other four, and collapsing the two produced a page
that opened with a wall of caveats. *Copy all of it and fill the five with something* — every
substitute is a claim about standing that this project cannot make, and it would be untrue in the one
register a reader cannot check. *Renumber §3.2 freely* — three shipped components cite these numbers
by section, so the numbers are API; the two that moved were updated in this commit.
— **Effect.** §3.1's expectation-setting goal is unchanged and now met **inside** their structure
rather than in place of it: Solid 2.0 and Panda are still above the fold, as one spec line under the
install command instead of three cards. `plan.md` §0's parity sentence **moves out of the hero** into
the parity section — Q3 is settled *prominent*, and prominent is not first: it now sits in the one
section that also shows what the parity gap costs.
— **Settled.** S5, 2026-08-10, against the built page. Reverses `docs-plan.md` §3.1 and §3.2 as
written at P8; §3.3's four rendered obligations are unchanged, which is the check that the rework
dropped nothing the page owed.
— **Reasoning.** `docs-plan.md` §3.1, §3.2; `docs-site.md` §2.2.

**D-176 · `legal.md`'s 286 dead citations are repointed to where each rule actually lives, and the
records that name the document are left standing**
— **Decision.** `legal.md` was deleted at `1eb56e8` and 286 citations across 91 tracked files went on
pointing at it. Each is repointed to the surviving home of the rule it cited, not deleted: the
expression tier, *depend do not vendor* and the hope-ui carry-over rule to **`CLAUDE.md`,
*Reference use***; the attribution mechanism to **`CLAUDE.md`'s five obligations**, by number; the
fork to **`zag-solid-adapter.md` §1**; the upstream-tracking policy to **`testing.md` §11**; the
brand, repo and maintainer-contact decisions to **D-01, D-02, D-168**; the licence table to
**`NOTICE.md`**, which is the surviving per-project licence record. **21 references survive on
purpose** — they are records of what a document said at a past gate, not pointers a reader follows.
— **Rejected.** *Restore `legal.md`* — the deletion was deliberate and `CLAUDE.md` was already
reconciled to it: it says *"Ten documents in `__internal__/`"* and *"Attribution has no document:
the rule is above, the registry is `attribution.config.ts`."* Reversing that is a decision, and
nothing found in this sweep argues for it. *Delete the citations and keep the sentences* — the
sentence *"the header is load-bearing"* without its pointer is a rule with nowhere arguing it, which
is the state `definition-of-done.md` §7 exists to prevent. *Rewrite the historical records too* — a
**Reasoning** line records where an argument lived when the decision was made; repointing it at
`CLAUDE.md` would claim P1 reasoned from a file that did not yet say this.
— **Effect.** **286 → 24, and every remaining one is inside `__internal__`.** Code, configs,
workflows, `NOTICE.md` and the four package notices are clean. Three of the survivors were not
records but *live* explanations illustrated with the dead document — `testing/8.03`'s sharding
worked example and its stale tightest-three table, and `testing/8.01`'s unnumbered-heading blind
spot — and those are repointed at live subjects, `component-blueprint.md` §11 and `decisions-ledger.md`
§6.1. **The hazard the sweep had to design around** is the rule `scripts/lib/skill-pointers.mjs`
states: *an anchor is governed by the nearest backticked `*.md` before it on the same line.* Fifteen
lines cited two or more anchors on one document, so replacing the first alone would have silently
re-pointed the rest at the new document — `` `legal.md` §1.3, §2.3 `` becoming
`` `zag-solid-adapter.md` §1, §2.3 ``. Those fifteen were rewritten first, by hand.
— **Settled.** S5, 2026-08-10, by grep over every tracked file.
— **Reasoning.** `CLAUDE.md`, *Reference use, and the expression tier*; **D-171** (the citation
resolver, run once and not promoted).

**D-177 · `mergeConfigs` is retired as the documented merge form — it is unreachable from the
install page, and the claim it carried about `theme` was backwards** ⟲
> **The spread this entry lands on is itself superseded, and `chakraConfig` no longer exists.**
> `98b921c` replaced it with `defineChakraConfig(overrides)`, which returns the whole `Config` and
> owns the merge, so there is nothing left to spread. The name is kept below because S5 decided
> about `chakraConfig`; `defineChakraConfig` did not exist yet.
— **Decision.** The six places telling consumers to write `mergeConfigs([chakraConfig(), { … }])`
are replaced by a named call and a spread of what it returned — `const chakra = chakraConfig()`, then
`presets: [...(chakra.presets ?? []), myPreset]`. **`theme` leaves the hazard list**: written under
`theme.extend` it merges and costs nothing, which is the opposite of what all six places said. The
list that remains is `presets`, and `staticCss` only when `responsive` was passed.
— **Rejected.** *Keep `mergeConfigs` and add `@pandacss/config` to the install step* — it makes a
second install a prerequisite of the merge form, and `mergeConfigs` returns `any`, so the reader
trades the `Config` types `chakraConfig()` exists to supply for a helper that does what a spread
already does. *Document `mergeConfigs` as an option beside the spread* — two forms where one works
is the shape that produced this bug: a reader picks the one nearer the top and neither sentence gets
re-measured. *Fix only `installation.mdx` and `config.ts`* — the two consumer-visible sites are
rendered **from** `plan.md` §3.4 and `docs-plan.md` §4.1/§6.1, so the wrong sentence would be
restored by the next pass that follows the spec. *Say `theme` is a hazard anyway, defensively* — a
warning about a safe operation is a warning readers learn to skip.
— **Effect.** The install page keeps its *"two things about that, and no more"* budget: the second
thing becomes a snippet instead of a sentence, which is shorter to read and cannot be
mis-transcribed. `plan.md` §3.4 carries the measured table and `docs-plan.md` §6.1 owns the
`staticCss` half, because that hazard is only reachable through §3.8's `responsive` opt-in — with
`responsive` omitted, `chakraConfig()` returns no `staticCss` for a re-declare to destroy. **The
measurement is here and not on the pages**: five `panda cssgen` runs over a scratch consumer config
— `...chakraConfig()` alone (tokens and recipes survive), `presets` re-declared (all gone), `theme`
with `extend` (all survive, consumer token present), `theme` without (about half the tokens gone,
recipes intact), and `presets` spread back in beside `theme.extend` (all survive). Both published
snippets were typechecked under the docs app's own tsconfig, which types against `dist`.
— **Settled.** S5, 2026-08-10, by five `panda cssgen` runs and `node -e` over `@pandacss/dev`'s
export list. Reverses the `theme` half of `plan.md` §3.4 as written at P3.
— **Reasoning.** `plan.md` §3.4, §3.8; `docs-plan.md` §4.1, §6.1.
